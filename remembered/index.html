Not a page count — a roll of names

Remembered

Every name here was a real person, held enslaved in Puerto Rico, recorded in Spain's own 1872 colonial registry — the same document archived in full elsewhere on this site. This page exists for one reason: so they are named, not just counted. They get their flowers here.

Read this before the list

Honest about how this list was made

These names come from machine reading (OCR) of 1870s handwritten Spanish colonial paperwork — genuinely difficult source material, not clean printed text. That means some names here are read correctly, and some almost certainly aren't — a stroke of a quill misread as one letter instead of another, a faded page, ink bled through from the other side. This is a first pass, not a finished, verified roll. The actual page images are archived in full in the Image Archive, and any name here can and should be checked against the real handwriting. If a name is wrong, it gets fixed — that matters more here than almost anywhere else on this site, because these are the only names some of these people have left in any record anywhere.

Each entry also lists who claimed to own them — not to honor that person, but because it's the second half of the same historical fact, and because in many entries it's the only other detail the record preserves alongside the name itself.

Puerto Rican-born, or brought from Africa

Where they were actually from

The registry asks this directly — every entry has its own "Natural de" (born in) field. Counted across the whole 715-page District 1 register: 664 people are recorded as born in Puerto Rico itself, born into slavery because Spanish colonial law made a child born to an enslaved mother enslaved too, and 149 people are recorded as born in Africa — real, individually named survivors of the transatlantic crossing itself, still alive and still enslaved in this one district alone, one year before abolition. A few more are recorded as born in Venezuela and Curaçao. Nobody is recorded as born in Spain — Spain organized and profited from this system, but was never itself a source of enslaved people.

Matching that birthplace field back to a specific name below, one by one, is a harder problem than counting the totals — the handwriting and page layout don't always keep a person's name and their own birthplace field close enough together for automated reading to link them with confidence. Where a match was solid enough to trust, it's tagged below. Most entries don't have a tag yet, not because that person's birthplace is unknown to the record, but because it hasn't been confidently linked back to their name by this pass — another real, honest gap, closeable by reading the actual page images in the Image Archive.
