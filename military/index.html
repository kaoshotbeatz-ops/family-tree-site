Confirmed service

Backed by primary military and government records, not family memory alone.

U.S. Army · Vietnam

Orlando Cruz Arocho

25 Jan 1947 – 19 Apr 2000 · Arecibo, Puerto Rico

Son of Rosa María Arocho Hernández and Víctor Cruz Feliciano — first cousin once removed on Angel A. Marrero's side of the family. The U.S. Veterans' Gravesites index names him directly: Pv2, U.S. Army, Vietnam. He's buried at Cementerio Municipal in Arecibo.

Nothing about his service had ever been passed down as family story before this project found the record — it was the Veterans' Gravesites index itself that first revealed he'd served at all. Five more independent sources (an obituary, the Social Security Death Index, the Social Security Applications and Claims Index, and the VA's own BIRLS Death File) all agree on his date of death, and the Applications and Claims Index independently reconfirms his parents.

U.S. Veterans' Gravesites ca. 1775-2019; U.S. Obituary Collection 1930-Current; U.S. Social Security Death Index 1935-2014; U.S. Social Security Applications and Claims Index 1936-2007; U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs BIRLS Death File 1850-2020. Confirmed 2026-07-23.
U.S. Army · Korean War

Robert Marrero Montalvo

27 Mar 1925 – 3 Aug 1963 · Arecibo, PR → Farmingdale, NY

Amado's brother — one of Juan Ramón Marrero and María Montalvo Ramos's children, Thelma's uncle, Omar's great-granduncle. The U.S. Veterans' Gravesites record puts exact dates on his service: enlisted 30 March 1951, served until 18 March 1953, a Private First Class in the U.S. Army during the Korean War — the record's own note reads "Pfc Us Army Korea."

He died young, at thirty-eight, and was interred at Long Island National Cemetery in Farmingdale, New York — Section 2J, Site 4253 — a national military cemetery, a long way from Sabana Hoyos and Arecibo, the world his parents and siblings never left. No spouse or children turned up in any record checked.

Puerto Rico, Civil Registrations, 1885-2001 (birth record, Arecibo); U.S., Veterans' Gravesites, ca. 1775-2019 (naming exact service dates, cemetery, and plot). Confirmed 2026-07-25; full chapter in the book.

Service unconfirmed by record

Angel A. Marrero

b. 24 Jan 1954 · the man who raised Omar

Omar's testimony, recorded here as fact per this project's standing rule: Angel served in the U.S. Army during the Vietnam era, in high-level communications — signals work, not a name that shows up on casualty rolls or gravesite indexes. Before the Army he went to East Rockaway High School, which the paper trail independently supports: a 1972 East Rockaway school yearbook entry for "Angel Amado Marrero" sits in his own Ancestry hint queue, putting him exactly where the family says he was, in exactly the year a 1954-born man would be finishing school and becoming draft-eligible.

But it's honest to say plainly: no military record has surfaced for him yet. His own Ancestry hint queue (9 hints, all reviewed) has no service, draft, or discharge record among them, and a direct search of every U.S. military collection reachable — muster rolls, BIRLS, Veterans' Gravesites, enlistment and service records — under his name and birthdate returned no match.

That's not evidence against him — it's how the record system works. Every searchable Vietnam-era index this project can reach (Veterans' Gravesites, BIRLS, Fold3's rolls — all checked again 2026-07-31) only lists veterans who have died. A living veteran's service file sits at the National Personnel Records Center in St. Louis, and only the veteran or next of kin can request it. The real path to the paper: Angel himself (or Omar, with Angel's OK) files a free Standard Form 180 or an online request at milConnect/eVetRecs for his DD-214 — that one document would give branch, dates, rank, and his communications specialty, and would finish this page's entry for good.

Family testimony (Omar Huertas). Ancestry hint queue reviewed 2026-07-31 (9/9, no service records). Direct Military-category search (U.S. Navy Muster Rolls 1949-1971, U.S. Marine Corps Muster Rolls 1798-1958, VA BIRLS 1850-2020) returned no exact match for Angel A. Marrero b. 24 Jan 1954.

Registered for the draft

Seven more family members registered for the WWI and WWII drafts — real cards with their own handwriting, physical descriptions, and addresses. No deployment record has surfaced for any of them yet ("not found yet" is not proof none exists). Puerto Rican men became draft-eligible in 1917, the same year Congress made them citizens — without a vote in the government sending them.

Died in war

Answered plainly, because Omar asked plainly.

No one in this family is documented to have died in war. Every record checked says the same thing: those who served came home. Orlando came back from Vietnam and lived to 2000. Robert came back from Korea in 1953 — he died young, at thirty-eight in 1963, but a decade after his discharge, and no record ties his death to his service. Miguel Cardona Reyes registered for the WWII draft and died at twenty-two in 1942, but in Arecibo, of civilian causes, never having been called up. In a century that took Puerto Rican sons by the thousands — the 65th Infantry alone lost hundreds in Korea — this family sent its men and got every one of them back. That is worth recording as deliberately as any loss would have been.
