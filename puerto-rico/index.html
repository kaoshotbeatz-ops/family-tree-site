🌳 You're driving straight through the family's ground today. The whole day sits inside the towns this family actually comes from — no detours needed, just know what you're looking at:

Bayamón (PR-22, ~20 min in): where Ramón "Moncho" Ceballo Vargas was born in 1905, where he married Alicia in 1932, and where Rosa (1933) and her sister Iris (1936) were born.
Arecibo (the cemetery stop): the deepest hub — the Marrero, Arocho, and Cardona lines all ran through here for generations; Thelma was born here in 1943, and Iraida, Miguel Cardona Reyes, Amado, and Milagros all lived here.
Utuado (Caguana): coffee country where the family's Torres Núñez half-branch farmed on Río Prieto.
Lares (~15 min west of Caguana on PR-111, optional 30-min round trip): Martín Arocho y Román raised eleven children here in the 1800s, Guillermo was born here in 1890, and María Hernández y Núñez's line ran through Río Prieto — the single oldest documented family town on the Arocho side. The town plaza and the Grito de Lares church make a quick, meaningful photo stop if the day is running ahead of schedule.

Añasco, Moca, and Las Marías (the very oldest ground, 1700s–1820s) are further west than this route reaches — saved for a future trip.

Arecibo Observatory (Arecibo C3 STEM Center)

Bo. Esperanza, PR-625, Arecibo
Checked directly against AC3's own site (areciboc3.org) — the visitor center has no general-public hours in August 2026. Tickets must be reserved online 24 hrs ahead, no walk-ups, and their own page says public tours "will resume in September." So going inside is off the table this trip.

But you can still see it. The drive up PR-625 dead-ends at the observatory gate, and travelers report you can pull off at the turn a few hundred yards before the gate and see the remaining structure from the road — the two giant support towers and the edge of the dish bowl in the sinkhole, free, no ticket, ~10–15 min stop. The dish platform itself collapsed in 2020, so the towers-and-rim view from outside is honestly most of what the paid platform shows anyway.

The drive cost stands either way: ~48 min of extra driving round-trip from the Cueva Ventana→Ponce route. As a quick photo stop instead of a 90-min visit, it lands you in Ponce around 5:45–6:00 PM — very doable.

Observatorio de Arecibo (Arecibo C3 STEM Center)

Bo. Esperanza, PR-625, Arecibo
Verificado directamente con la propia página de AC3 (areciboc3.org) — el centro de visitantes no tiene horario para el público general en agosto de 2026. Los boletos hay que reservarlos en línea con 24 horas de anticipación, no se aceptan visitas sin reserva, y su propia página dice que los recorridos públicos "se reanudarán en septiembre". Así que entrar queda descartado en este viaje.

Pero todavía se puede ver. La subida por la PR-625 termina en el portón del observatorio, y los viajeros reportan que uno se puede orillar en la curva a unos cientos de yardas antes del portón y ver lo que queda de la estructura desde la carretera — las dos torres gigantes de soporte y el borde del plato en el sumidero, gratis, sin boleto, una parada de ~10 a 15 min. La plataforma del plato colapsó en 2020, así que la vista de las torres y el borde desde afuera es, honestamente, casi todo lo que se ve desde la plataforma pagada de todos modos.

El costo del manejo se mantiene igual: ~48 min de manejo extra ida y vuelta desde la ruta Cueva Ventana→Ponce. Como parada rápida de fotos en vez de una visita de 90 minutos, te deja en Ponce cerca de las 5:45–6:00 PM — bien factible.
